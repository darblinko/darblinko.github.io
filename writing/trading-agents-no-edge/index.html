My LLM trading agents found no edge, and how I know

I tested three layers of an LLM trading research system and none showed an edge. 0 of 8 swing setup families passed the Stage A gate, 0 of 6 directional entry models passed the b17 gates, and the signals the LLM agent approved were direction-correct less often (45.9% of 37) than the signals it rejected (49.6% of 115).

These results are research on historical data and on 13 recorded sessions from 2026-05-26 to 2026-06-11. They are not investment advice, and nothing here is a recommendation to trade any security or option. Backtest and replay results do not predict future results.

Each set has its own unit and its own gate, so no figure is pooled across sets. Stage A removes duplicate and overlapping trades before any check, so one long hold cannot count as several events. The LLM comparison scores the signals the agent declined with the same rule as the ones it executed, which gives the executed set a baseline drawn from the same sessions.

The repository's design places a look-ahead leakage audit before any gate. Its results tables do not record whether the Stage A and b17 runs went through that audit.

How the gates were set, and what the repository records about when

Stage A

The Stage A thresholds are the defaults of GateRules. A family passes only when all six checks pass.

CheckRule in code
min_eventsAt least 150 distinct events
distinct_monthsEvents in at least 24 distinct calendar months
expectancyMean R at least 0.15
bootstrap_ciLower bound of the 90% bootstrap interval of mean R above 0; 5,000 resamples of two-month blocks, seed 0
recent_yearsMean R above 0 in at least 2 of the recent years (2019, 2020 and 2021 in this run)
controlR minus the matched control's R: mean above 0 and 90% interval lower bound above 0, with control values for at least 90% of events and at least 150 of them

The bootstrap draws whole two-month blocks because a hold can run up to 30 sessions, longer than a calendar month. A Bonferroni-corrected verdict for 8 families is computed beside the gate and is informational; the gate does not use it.

The docstring of GateRules reads "Stage A gate thresholds, fixed before any out-of-sample run". The entry filters in StageAConfig carry the same sentence, and their 2.0 reward-to-risk floor is recorded as matching the live quality gate's floor. The recorded Stage A result is the in-sample run, whose output was created on 2026-10-12. The repository gives no date for the thresholds and does not record whether they were set before the in-sample results were seen. Its git history cannot settle the order: the staged history begins with one import commit, ebdf4e7, on 2026-10-28.

The gate is tested against two synthetic families with known answers. On random-walk bars at seed 0, 519 random entries have mean R -0.035 and fail four of the six checks. A family that enters only when the close five bars ahead is higher has 486 events, mean R 0.749 and a 90% interval of [0.651, 0.847], and passes all six. The gate can therefore return a pass on a family with an edge, and it cannot tell a real edge from look-ahead.

b17

The four b17 gates are recorded as column names: PR-AUC above baseline, net expectancy above 0, profit factor above 1 and win rate above 40%. A model passes when all four pass. As recorded in the source, the PR-AUC gate requires the lower 95% bound to exceed 0 and does not compare with the class base rate. The repository does not record when the b17 gates were set relative to the experiment.

The LLM entry decision did not select better signals

Over 13 sessions, 152 signals triggered. The LLM agent executed 37 and gated out 115, declining 75.7% of them. The executed signals were direction-correct over the next 15 minutes at 0.459, the gated-out signals at 0.496 and all signals at 0.487. Taking every triggered signal would have scored higher on this measure than taking only the ones the agent approved.

The difference is small against the sample. The rates correspond to 17 of 37 and 57 of 115 signals. For this write-up I computed the standard error of the 0.037 difference as 0.094, so the gap is 0.39 standard errors. The 95% Wilson intervals, 0.31 to 0.62 for executed and 0.41 to 0.59 for gated out, overlap. The source did not compute intervals.

The agent labelled 1 signal high confidence and did not execute it. It executed 29 of 112 medium-confidence signals and 8 of 39 low-confidence ones. The 30 completed trades won at a rate of 0.467: 13 call trades at 0.692 and 17 put trades at 0.294. The source reports a win rate of 0.429 for executed trades at both medium and low confidence.

Inference: in this sample the go or wait decision removed three quarters of the signals without raising the direction-correct rate of the remainder. The sample can show the absence of a measurable lift. It cannot show that the decision was harmful, because the 3.7-point gap is within sampling error. The measure is a proxy: it ignores option decay and the exit, so it is not the trade outcome.

Signals and rates: data/results/expectancy_gate_lift.csv rows 2 to 4. Window: data/results/README.md lines 39 and 40; proxy definition and its limits: lines 41 to 43.

Computed: 115 / 152 = 0.757. 0.459 x 37 = 16.98 and 0.496 x 115 = 57.04, rounded to 17 and 57. Standard error sqrt(0.459 x 0.541 / 37 + 0.496 x 0.504 / 115) = 0.094; 0.037 / 0.094 = 0.39. Wilson score intervals at z = 1.96 for 17 of 37 and 57 of 115.

What I concluded

  1. None of the 14 candidates in the first two sets qualifies for a next stage. No Stage A family passed the in-sample gate, and the repository records no out-of-sample Stage A result. No b17 model met all four gates.
  2. A classification pass did not translate into expectancy. Each b17 model cleared the PR-AUC gate, and each model's 95% net expectancy interval lies below 0 bps. A future PR-AUC gate should compare with the class base rate; as recorded it compares with 0.
  3. Inference: over 13 sessions the LLM's go decision did not pick signals with better short-term direction than the ones it declined. The measurement gives no support for keeping the LLM decision as an entry filter, and it is too small to show that the filter subtracts.
  4. Inference: the Stage A negatives carry information because the same gate passes a synthetic family with built-in edge (mean R 0.749) and fails random entries (mean R -0.035). The same test shows that the gate cannot see look-ahead, so a pass would have needed the leakage audit before it counted.

Limits of these results

  • Sample sizes. The LLM comparison rests on 37 executed and 115 gated-out signals and 30 completed trades over 13 sessions; the source computed no intervals for these rates. Two Stage A families have 82 and 32 events.
  • Periods. Stage A is in-sample only, 2016-01-01 to 2021-12-31. The b17 data period is not recorded in the repository. The LLM sessions run from 2026-05-26 to 2026-06-11.
  • Costs. The staged Stage A code computes R with no slippage or commission, and the cost treatment of the private run is not recorded. b17 expectancy is reported net, and its cost assumptions are not recorded. The direction-correct proxy ignores option decay and exits.
  • Exported and rewritten pieces. The result tables were exported once from private research repositories and cannot be regenerated from this repository. The code runs on synthetic data only. The walk-forward module recomputes features from OHLCV and does not reproduce the private metrics. The review apps replay a synthetic session, and the example prompt record is fictional.
  • Not tested or not published. Stage A out-of-sample; the entry rules of the families and models; PR-AUC against the class base rate; the identity and prompt version of the LLM that made the entry decisions, which the tables do not record. The live prompts, the LLM pipeline and the order execution code are not included. Dollar results, per-trade returns, equity curves, Sharpe ratios and drawdowns were not exported.
  • Inconsistencies in the exported tables.
    • The setup family rows of expectancy_trades.csv sum to 28 trades, against 30 overall.
    • A win rate of 0.429 cannot be a share of 29 or of 8 executed trades (0.429 x 29 = 12.44; 0.429 x 8 = 3.43). Inference: it equals 12 of 28, the win count of those 28 family rows, and may be one aggregate reported under both confidence levels.
    • 37 signals were executed and 30 round trips completed. The repository does not reconcile the two counts.
